Session 6 - Not at This Time, Your Honor

Session 6 Image Log

Where You Left Off

Four of you were being marched down to the holding cells in the Switchback, charged with Hollis's murder on Kerra's complaint. Celienne was free, alone, and looking for a stable or a barn to hide in.

What Happened

Mira is gone.

She heard what happened to you: the market, the guards, the carnage. She didn't want to wait and see how it all ended.

"Whatever this city is, she decided that this fight wasn't hers, and she took the road out of Halvyr and did not leave word about where she was going."

The door is still open, if she ever wants to come back through it.

"Name. Spell it. Everything on the table. All of it. Pockets turned."

Intake was flat and thorough. Someone read your lives back as a list:

"Greatsword, one. Daggers, four. Purse, coin…"

The woman running the building wore silver boots. Nobody told you her name, so you gave her one of your own: the silver-booted bitch. She picked up the wax impression of Kerra's key, looked at it, and slipped it into her own coat. It never went into the log. You all saw it, and none of you said a word.

You were split into two cells. Pip and Athwyn in one, Dhar and Elodie across the hall, fifteen feet away and close enough to talk. Stone walls, barred doors, a bucket each, windows set too high to reach.

That evening the old jailer took you out one at a time to a closed courtyard to wash. He gave you water and a thin blanket. He hummed while he worked.

"The blanket's thin, but at least it's dry."

Elodie tried to stare him down. He chuckled to himself. Dhar asked about him: he'd been there about fifteen years, he was from just outside of town, and the limp came from a farming accident when he was young.

"Not that I share with inmates, no. In fifteen years, you learn not to do that."

Pip asked about the South Market attackers: they'd likely be charged with assault, maybe attempted murder. Your gear comes back "if you're found innocent." He wouldn't say where it was. Athwyn asked about representation (an advocate, "you'll get to meet him in the morning") and then asked him straight out for help.

"I've never had a criminal in here yet that said, yep, I did it."

He was going on the orders he'd been given by the council.

Somewhere at the far end of the block, a man was moaning in his cell. He did not sound well.

Across from Pip and Athwyn, a man came to his bars.

"Quickgrin… Look where you washed up."

Salt Ledger. The syndicate. And instead of a fight, Pip gave him a conversation.

"I was sent to find you. You know why. That book."

Pip told him she'd burned it.

"Why would you burn it?"

Because it was dangerous, and if it got into the wrong hands people could get in trouble for it.

"You were going to be paid for it. That's between you and the boss."

Then Pip floated a deal: make her enemies her friends, for now. She has sticky fingers and she's quick. If they made a distraction, she might liberate the keys. He said the others would play along. He'd heard of the Split Ledger but never worked with them. As for the council:

"They're a bunch of bureaucrats as far as I'm concerned. Generally we end up with contracts to do something for one or the other. I don't know any of them. I've done jobs for a few of them indirectly."

Across the two cells, you argued it out. Athwyn could cover your escape. Pip had the fingers. But you'd lose your gear, and Elodie was not leaving her circlet behind. Kerra would just send people after you, and you'd end up right back in here. Celienne needed time.

You decided to sit tight and fight the charge.

Out in the city, Celienne put on a plain woman's face in plain brown clothes and went to the Hearthleaf. She told Elodie the plan: get everyone's things, and sneak the cart out after dark.

She slept the night in the alley behind it, twenty feet from a rented room with her own things in it. A patrol came through and missed her.

At half past four she went over the back wall. It took about an hour of falling off it.

By half past five, Rowan had heard her. She came out of the kitchen.

"What are you doing here?… You realize the Watch is after you."

Celienne played the fool. She'd left those women behind. They were up to no good. She didn't know what was going on. And the wall?

"Well, I was a little drunk and I didn't want you to see it."

Rowan believed her, or chose not to fight about it, and let her gather her things. The Watch arrived a few minutes later anyway.

Celienne bagged the party's gear, went out the window, and took the cart and the horses, Ketchup and Mustard, with about a five-minute lead. On the road she put the plain brown disguise back on. The guards looked right at her and the cart, and kept looking past.

Back in the cells, around three in the morning, the humming stopped.

The jailer came down the corridor with a man in a hooded cloak. He unlocked the end cell next to Pip and Athwyn's and walked back toward his desk. Halfway there he looked back over his shoulder at the cloaked man. Then he kept walking.

Pip was awake. Elodie was awake. She weighed stepping out of the cell, and couldn't make it work without her focus. She rattled Dhar's bucket to wake her instead.

From the end cell came a muffled struggle. Through the open door you could see the cloak, bent over the bench, a hand over the prisoner's mouth.

Then the sound stopped.

The cloaked man left the way he came, his face never showing. None of you called out.

The dead man wasn't one of the five from South Market, but he was dressed like the others in the syndicate cells. You don't know who he was.

Athwyn found someone who did see it all. A rat, a boy with big teeth, came out of the dead man's cell, and Athwyn talked to it.

"Saw the cloak man come in and slit the throat of the man on the bench."

The dead man had been there three or four days. He'd been hollering back and forth with "the guys across the hall", who wanted to know whether he'd "said anything." The name Salt Ledger came up. And when Athwyn asked whether anyone had talked about Kerra:

"The fellow that got murdered did, yeah."

Put that next to a throat cut in the night and you drew your own conclusion: he'd worked for Kerra, and Kerra is cleaning up loose ends.

The jailer, according to the rat, is "the lead head guard here at night," and it hadn't seen him let anyone else in that day. Your gear is in a storage room down the hall from the main desk. Pip offered it bread to go and look: several chests, and it couldn't get into any of them. And the silver-booted woman?

"She's the head boss of this building."

Did she work with Kerra?

"I'm just a rat."

Pip had eaten half the bread before she handed it over.

"Hey, you ate half of it."

It bit her on the ankle and left.

In the morning there was a commotion at shift change. The end cell was empty, and the body was gone. Across the hall, the syndicate men had their own epitaph for him:

"He failed at his task. His lips were too loose. Loose lips sink ships."

They said they had suspicions about who did it.

Around seven that morning, Celienne walked into the Iron Bend without a disguise.

"Your friends are in a Switchback cell for killing Neris's messenger. And you walk in here. Talk fast."

So she talked. You'd been brought to Halvyr with promises of good work and good pay, and found the whole system corrupt, rigged and awful. You wanted to do something about it. And Hollis:

"The loss of Hollis was an unfortunate accident. We were on the wrong side and we didn't know."

She asked to keep helping the Split Ledger make things better for the people here. Marrick didn't soften.

"I pass word. That's what I do. You already owe me. This is one more on the slate, not less."

Neris was no warmer.

"Your friends are in a council cell for killing my messenger. On the second chair's complaint. Using my words. I don't walk into council rooms. Not for her, not for you."

But a written recant could be done, for a price. Either finish the records job ("I need that job done before it's too late") or bring her something of Kerra's: letterhead, her seal, something in her own hand that shows how she has things redirected.

Celienne took the records job. In return, Neris promised paperwork recanting her statement, though she couldn't say whether it would matter at this point, and a blank Watch release form with a passable seal.

"It'll pass a tired sergeant. It won't pass Serra Vale."

That same morning, the four of you were brought to the lunch room to meet your advocate, Perrin Sallow.

"I've been appointed, which means the court's paying me, which means I've been paid very little. Murder, before a council panel, with a second chair presenting… You've annoyed someone."

"Twenty-five gold a sitting buys you an advocate who reads the file. Fifty buys one who argues."

You signed a retainer against the purse they'd taken off you at intake.

The search for witnesses had already begun. Hollis's mother was found almost at once. Neris was not found, not yet. Rowan was never in doubt.

You asked Perrin for witnesses of your own: the tea-house maids, to speak for your character, and the man at the shipyards who'd hired you to clear out the fire beetles. He'll have them for the trial, but not for this first hearing. You also asked him to stretch it out as long as humanly possible and bog down the bureaucracy.

When he asked for your side, you told him you were in the wrong place at the wrong time, set upon by brigands, and defended yourselves. He pressed about the brick and the chase.

"He was alive when we left him."

You told him one of the maids had seen Hollis sitting in the garden afterward.

"Interesting. This is definitely not an open-and-shut case… You have some more chance than some that I see here."

Across the city, around one in the afternoon, Celienne hitched Ketchup near the Gilded Stag and became somebody else: a records clerk she'd watched leave for lunch.

She walked straight in behind the desk.

"You're back quick from lunch."

She spent half an hour in the wrong file room. Then she found the file to take in four minutes and the file to change in thirteen, with about seven minutes left on her disguise. Her first pass at the alteration was passable but rough, the kind of thing whoever wrote the file would spot. She went back over it until it was a fairly decent job.

"Having a rough day today, Janine?"

"I can't wrap my head around things today. I didn't sleep well last night."

Then Janine walked out the door.

Celienne never opened the file she took. All she knows is that the one she changed now sends goods toward Tallwood.

At two that afternoon, the four of you stood before a council panel.

"…and one still at large, Celienne Duskveil, did cause the death of Hollis, a messenger of the Ash Ward, in the streets of the Hearthleaf. Four accused present, fifth is sought."

The panel: Arven Talric on the end; Edric Corban, first chair, appointed to the council before this king took power; Sera Carn, the king's sister, in a purple dress; and Velra. A magistrate you weren't given a name for presided.

Kerra presented the complaint herself.

"A young man has died. The accused brought the woman who was with them to the Hearthleaf and left him outside. In front of Mistress Bell, that woman accused them of killing him. Mistress Bell will say so. That is the complaint."

She stopped there. She never mentioned the box.

Perrin earned his twenty-five gold.

"The accuser is not before you. She is Split Ledger, and she is not likely to be. The body has been burned. There is no wound for this panel to examine."

He asked that the accuser be produced, and that the Watch's report on the South Market attack the day before your arrest be entered into the record.

Then there was the Stand, warded so that anyone who lies on it, "they'll know about it." It glows red.

"Don't go up there unless you mean every word."

None of you went up there.

Asked whether the accused had anything to say, Elodie answered for all of you:

"Not at this time, Your Honor."

"Witnesses to be sought: Mistress Rowan Bell, the woman who made the accusation, the mother of the deceased, and any who saw the deceased that day. The Watch, through Lieutenant Vale's office, will make inquiry. The accused remain held. The fifth accused is to be taken. This panel sits again in seven days to hear witnesses."

By half past four, Celienne was back at the Iron Bend. Neris was pleased with the file and took it from her directly. She handed over the blank release form.

Back in the cells, the message reached Elodie:

"So I have good news. I fulfilled the last task we had for Neris. I still owe them a lot, but Neris has given me a release form for you guys, as well as a recant of her statement."

The less eyes on the paperwork, the better, Celienne said. She was out of spells and couldn't act until tomorrow. Elodie passed the news along right in front of the guards:

"Have faith. You never know what might drop from the sky."

You described the layout of the block to Celienne. She hid in a back alley near the cells without a disguise, tail tucked, and nobody found her. You waited, and slept on stone, more than one night.

Celienne filled in Neris's blank form with a careful hand. It was a good forgery.

At five in the morning, an hour before the night shift ended, Janine came back, dressed up this time because she was going out on the town. She handed the papers to the night jailer, Rulden.

"This is very unusual. Weird timing too. Why are you here at five in the morning?"

She'd been asked to deliver the release papers for the four accused, she said, and a recant had been filed. Neris had already sent hers ahead, signed and sealed, to the courthouse.

Rulden needed his boss. Celienne waited an hour in the kitchen with a cup of tea.

The silver-booted woman arrived: Lieutenant Serra Vale. She looked over the paperwork. Word came from the court that a recant had been filed.

She let all four of you go, with your gear.

You chose your side out loud: stick with the Split Ledger for now. They just got you out. They want to take down the council, and you're all on board with that.

Then you chose to get out of Halvyr. Brindlemark, where after the caravan job they probably see you as fairly neutral. But first, back to Neris, to say thank you and ask whether she had anything for you on the way.

She did. At Brindlemark there are wine casks marked for the Gilded Stag. Re-mark three or four of them, not all ("that would be suspicious"), so they ship down to the stockyards where her people can collect them.

"We don't want anything direct to come to the Iron Bend either."

She gave you a drawing of the mark to chalk on. Brindlemark is a couple of days out with the cart.

On the road you told Celienne about the cloaked man and the death in the cell, and she told you how she got you out.

You camped in a clearing near a cave. A foul smell drifted out of it, and it was more than a dead animal.

Athwyn asked a squirrel. You named it Pickles, and it got a peanut for its trouble.

"I wouldn't go in that cave if I were you… There's ogres in there."

Two of them, it said. Aggressive and usually hungry. And what do ogres hate? Its best guess was cleanliness.

So you built the fire up high, left the horses and the cart out as bait, and hid in the trees.

"Lunch! Meat's back on the menu, boys!"

There were three of them, not two. The third came rumbling out of the brush halfway through the fight.

Pip shot from behind the cart. Elodie opened with a sustained arc of lightning that held the first ogre until it died. Dhar, carrying Celienne's inspiration, put her greatsword into the second for a huge hit. Celienne's cutting words turned a greatclub into a tree, and her laughter finally landed on the third one and put it on the ground. Athwyn burned them with fire bolts, then drew a blade of flame. Pip's arrow dropped the second ogre. The third swung so wild it hit its own dead partner, and the club stuck.

All three ogres went down. Not one of you was touched. The only casualty was a wheel on your cart, smashed by a greatclub.

The cave is still there, and nobody has gone in yet.

What You Learned

Someone has reach inside the Holding Cells. You watched the night jailer open a door for a killer and walk away.

The dead man talked about Kerra, the rat says. You've decided he worked for her, and that she had him silenced. Nobody has proved it.

The Split Ledger got you out, and they keep a tally. Celienne owes Marrick one more on the slate, and owes Neris a lot.

Pip told the syndicate the ledger is ash. Whether they believe her is another matter.

Where You Stand Now

All five of you are together again on the road to Brindlemark, armed and out of the cells, standing over three dead ogres outside a cave nobody has entered.

  • You're out on a release Celienne forged on Neris's blank form, delivered as Janine. Neris's recant is on file at the courthouse.
  • The panel ordered you held, and it sits again in seven days. You left the city before then.
  • Perrin Sallow is on retainer for twenty-five gold, and is looking for the maids and the man from the shipyards. He's been told Hollis was alive when you left him.
  • The key impression Serra Vale pocketed didn't come back with your gear.
  • Neris's job: chalk three or four Gilded Stag wine casks at Brindlemark so they ship to the stockyards.
  • The cart has a broken wheel, two days from anywhere.

The questions for next time:

What's in the cave?

Does a seven-day sitting carry on without the accused in the room?

Who sent the man in the cloak?
